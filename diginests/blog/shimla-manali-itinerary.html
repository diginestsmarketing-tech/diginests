Picture yourself driving through misty pine forests, winding mountain roads, charming colonial towns, endless apple orchards, and breathtaking Himalayan valleys. The Shimla–Manali circuit is one of India's most iconic mountain road trips, offering the perfect mix of natural beauty, adventure, culture, and regional cuisine.

Whether you are planning a family holiday, a romantic honeymoon, or an unforgettable road trip with friends, this comprehensive 7-day Shimla to Manali travel guide provides day-by-day itineraries, exact travel times, budget estimates, permit requirements, and local insider tips.

Detailed Day-by-Day Travel Itinerary

Day 1: Delhi/Chandigarh to Shimla & Evening Heritage Walk

Driving Distance: ~345 km from Delhi (7–8 hours) | ~115 km from Chandigarh (3–4 hours) via NH5.
Route: Delhi → Chandigarh → Parwanoo → Solan → Kandaghat → Shimla via NH5.

Your Himalayan journey begins today. As you leave behind the busy highways of Delhi or Chandigarh, the scenery gradually transforms into rolling hills, pine forests, and winding mountain roads. For travelers starting from Delhi, an early departure between 4:00 AM and 5:00 AM helps avoid plain traffic and allows you to reach Shimla before sunset.

Scenic Stops Along the Route

  • Parwanoo: The gateway to Himachal Pradesh. Stop for breakfast overlooking the timber trail valley and sample local fruit preserves.
  • Solan ("Mushroom City of India"): Located midway between Chandigarh and Shimla. Ideal for a tea break or a quick lunch at local roadside eateries.
  • Kandaghat: A peaceful hill stretch surrounded by dense pine groves, perfect for a quick photography break before the final climb into Shimla.

Arrival in Shimla

By late afternoon, you'll arrive in Shimla, the former summer capital of British India perched at 2,200 meters (7,200 feet). Check into your hotel, take a warm shower, and rest for an hour to acclimatize to the higher elevation before heading out.

Evening Heritage Walk Through Colonial Shimla

Head towards The Ridge and Mall Road, Shimla's two most iconic promenades. Because vehicles are strictly banned in this core zone, it provides a peaceful, open-air walking experience.

  • The Ridge: A wide-open plaza offering panoramic views of the surrounding peak ranges and the valley lights below.
  • Christ Church: Built in 1857, this Neo-Gothic landmark is illuminated by floodlights every evening, making it one of Shimla's most photographed spots.
  • Scandal Point & Gaiety Theatre: Walk past the historical Scandal Point to reach the Victorian-era Gaiety Theatre (built in 1887), which once hosted British theatrical performances.
  • Mall Road Shopping & Eats: Sample local snacks, fresh pastries from classic bakeries, or traditional Himachali Siddu (steamed stuffed bread served with ghee).

Essential Parking Information: Never drive directly toward Mall Road. Park at the Lift Parking on Cart Road, buy municipal lift tickets, and ride the elevator straight up to Mall Road to save a steep uphill trek.

Approximate Budget for Day 1

  • Budget: ₹2,500 – ₹4,000 (Guesthouse, local eateries, public transit/lift)
  • Mid-Range: ₹5,000 – ₹8,000 (3-Star hotel, cafe dining, cab expenses)
  • Luxury: ₹12,000+ (Heritage resort, fine dining)

Day 2: Kufri Excursion & Shimla’s Cultural Treasures

Total Driving Distance: ~40–50 km round trip.
Ideal Start Time: 8:00 AM.

Dedicate your second day to discovering the natural mountain landscapes, viewpoints, and historical heritage surrounding Shimla.

Morning: Excursion to Kufri (18 km from Shimla)

Located at 2,510 meters (8,235 feet), Kufri sits higher than Shimla and offers cooler temperatures, sweeping pine views, and snow activities during winter.

  • Himalayan Nature Park: Home to regional Himalayan wildlife, including the Himalayan Monal, musk deer, snow leopards, and barking deer. (Entry Fee: ~₹50–₹100).
  • Mahasu Peak: The highest point in Kufri, offering panoramic vistas of the Greater Himalayas. You can hike up the trail or hire a local pony guide.

Afternoon: Jakhoo Temple & Ropeway Experience

Return to Shimla and head to the Jakhoo Ropeway station. Ride the aerial cable car up to Jakhoo Hill (2,445 m)—the highest point in Shimla town.

  • Jakhoo Temple: Visit the ancient Hanuman Temple housing the famous 108-foot orange statue overlooking the city.
  • Monkey Caution: Jakhoo Hill is famous for its inquisitive monkey population. Keep food items hidden, secure your sunglasses, and store mobile phones inside your pockets or bags.

Evening: Historic Viceregal Lodge (Rashtrapati Niwas)

Located on Observatory Hill, this Jacobethan-style castle (built in 1888) served as the summer seat of the British Viceroy. Stroll through its manicured botanical lawns and take a guided heritage tour through the preserved teakwood interior rooms where historical partition discussions occurred.

Approximate Budget for Day 2

  • Ropeway Ticket: ~₹500 – ₹700 per person

Day 3: Scenic Drive from Shimla to Manali (via Mandi & Kullu Valley)

Driving Distance: ~250 km (7 to 8 hours depending on valley traffic).
Route: Shimla → Shalaghat → Bilaspur → Sundernagar → Mandi → Kullu → Manali (via NH21).

This transfer day takes you deep into central Himachal Pradesh, winding through mountain passes before dropping down into the Beas River Valley.

Highway Highlights & Recommended Stops

  • Sundernagar Lake: A man-made balancing reservoir that makes for a great morning tea and photo stop.
  • Mandi ("Varanasi of the Hills"): Stop near the historic stone temples along the Beas riverbank for lunch.
  • Pandoh Dam & Aut Tunnel: Stop at the Pandoh Dam lookout point before driving through the Aut Tunnel—the official entry point into the green Kullu Valley.
  • White-Water Rafting in Kullu: Stop at Babeli (near Kullu town) for white-water rafting on the Beas River (Cost: ₹800 – ₹1,500 per person depending on route length). You can also visit local handloom co-operatives to purchase authentic, certified Kullu shawls.

Evening Arrival in Manali

Arrive in Manali (2,050 meters) by early evening. Check into your hotel or riverside resort, enjoy a hot meal, and rest up for the mountain adventures ahead.

Approximate Budget for Day 3

  • Fuel / Cab Transfer: ₹3,500 – ₹5,500
  • Meals & Highway Dhaba Stops: ₹600 – ₹1,200 per person
  • Rafting Activity (Optional): ₹800 – ₹1,500 per person

Day 4: Old Manali, Cedar Forests & Local Cultural Sites

Dedicate this day to exploring the culture, historic wooden temples, riverside walks, and famous cafe culture of Manali.

Morning: Hadimba Temple, Vashisht Springs & Manu Temple

  • Hadimba Devi Temple: Built in 1553, this unique four-tiered wooden pagoda temple is tucked inside the towering cedar trees of Dhungri Van Vihar. (Entry: Free).
  • Vashisht Hot Water Springs: Located 3 km from main town, famous for its natural underground sulfur hot springs and ancient stone temples.
  • Manu Temple: Located at the top of Old Manali, dedicated to Sage Manu. The walk up offers charming views of old wooden Himachali homes and apple orchards.

Afternoon & Evening: Old Manali Cafe Hopping

Cross the iron bridge over the Manalsu River into Old Manali. Known for its bohemian vibe, cobblestone alleys, live music, and wooden houses, it provides a quiet escape from the busier commercial markets.

Top Cafe Recommendations

  • Cafe 1947: Located right beside the rushing river; perfect for trout, pasta, and wood-fired pizza.
  • Dylan’s Toasted & Roasted: Famous for fresh mountain coffee and homemade chocolate cookies.
  • Sunshine Cafe: Known for cozy interiors, hot chocolate, and comfort meals.

Shopping on Mall Road: Finish your evening by browsing the local handicraft market on main Mall Road for wooden carvings, woolen socks, and local apple jam.

Approximate Budget for Day 4

  • Sightseeing & Local Auto-Rickshaws: ₹400 – ₹800
  • Cafe Dining & Food: ₹1,000 – ₹2,000 per person
  • Shopping & Miscellaneous: Variable

Day 5: Solang Valley, Atal Tunnel & Trans-Himalayan Sissu (Lahaul Valley)

Driving Distance: Manali → Solang Valley (14 km) → Atal Tunnel (25 km) → Sissu (40 km total from Manali).
Ideal Start Time: 7:30 AM to beat valley traffic.

This high-altitude day trip takes you through the engineering marvel of the Atal Tunnel (9.02 km long) under the Pir Panjal range into the trans-Himalayan landscape of Lahaul.

Stop 1: Solang Valley Adventure Hub

Solang Valley is Northern India's premier mountain adventure ground.

Activities & Estimated Costs: Paragliding (₹1,500 – ₹3,200), Solang Ropeway Ride (₹700), Zorbing (₹500). During winter (Dec–Feb), Solang operates as a major skiing and snow-tubing resort.

Stop 2: Crossing the Atal Tunnel (10,040 feet)

Drive through the 9.02 km tunnel. As you emerge at the North Portal, notice the dramatic landscape change—from the lush green pine forests of Kullu to the dry, snow-dusted, craggy mountains of Lahaul Valley.

(Note: Stopping, parking, or taking photos inside the tunnel is strictly prohibited).

Stop 3: Sissu Waterfall & Chandra River

Explore Sissu village in Lahaul Valley:

  • Walk up the short trail to the Sissu Waterfall.
  • Stroll along the banks of the turquoise Chandra River.
  • Enjoy hot maggi, momos, and seabuckthorn tea at local wooden food stalls near Sissu Lake.

Approximate Budget for Day 5

  • Cab / Transport (Full-day Sissu trip): ₹3,500 – ₹5,500
  • Adventure Activities in Solang: ₹1,500 – ₹4,000 (Optional)

Day 6: Naggar Castle Heritage Excursion & Art Gallery

Driving Distance: ~20 km south of Manali along the Left Bank Road (45 minutes).

A quieter, culturally rich day trip situated away from the main tourist rush of town.

Naggar Castle

Built around 1460 AD by Raja Sidh Singh, this historical timber-and-stone castle features traditional Kath-Kuni architecture (alternating layers of heavy wood logs and stone without mortar). It offers commanding views over the Kullu Valley and the Beas River.

Entry Fee: ₹30 per person.

Lunch Tip: Dine at the Himachal Tourism restaurant located right inside the castle courtyard, or visit nearby riverside cafes to sample fresh local Kullu trout.

Nicholas Roerich Art Gallery

Located 1 km above Naggar Castle, this preserved estate was the home of the famed Russian painter and philosopher Nicholas Roerich. The gallery houses his original oil paintings capturing the spiritual light of the Himalayas.

Entry Fee: ₹50 per person.

Evening Relaxation

Head back to Manali town for your final evening. Enjoy a relaxed dinner in Old Manali or walk along the riverbanks to take in the mountain sunset.

Approximate Budget for Day 6

  • Transport / Taxi: ₹1,500 – ₹2,500
  • Entry Fees: ₹80 – ₹100 per person
  • Dining & Meals: ₹800 – ₹1,500 per person

Day 7: Manali to Delhi/Chandigarh Departure

Driving Time: ~7 to 8 hours to Chandigarh (300 km) | ~12 to 13 hours to Delhi (540 km).

Check out of your hotel early in the morning (around 6:00 AM) to ensure a smooth drive down the Kullu Valley back toward the plains.

Vehicle & Permit Checklist for Travelers

  • Rohtang Pass Permit Rules: If you choose to visit Rohtang Pass (instead of or in addition to the Atal Tunnel), note that vehicle access is strictly capped. Apply online via the official government portal at https://rohtangpermits.hp.gov.in/. The fee is ₹550 total (₹500 permit fee + ₹50 congestion charge). Your vehicle must have a valid Pollution Under Control (PUC) certificate and be under 10 years old.
  • Atal Tunnel Regulations: No permit is required for Indian nationals driving through the Atal Tunnel to Sissu. However, overtaking, stopping, or taking photographs inside the tunnel is monitored by speed cameras and subject to traffic fines.
  • Winter Driving Precautions: If traveling between late December and February, carry tire snow chains if you plan on driving a 2WD vehicle beyond Solang Valley or toward Kufri.
  • Mobile Networks: Airtel and Jio provide reliable 4G/5G coverage across most of the circuit, including Sissu and Lahaul Valley.

Conclusion: Planning Your Dream Himachali Journey

The Shimla–Manali circuit is far more than just a typical vacation route—it is a complete Himalayan experience that connects colonial history, ancient wooden architecture, thrilling high-altitude passes, and warm Himachali hospitality. Following this structured 7-day itinerary ensures you hit all the major landmarks while giving yourself enough time to soak in the mountain atmosphere without feeling rushed.

Whether you are sipping coffee along the riverbanks of Old Manali, taking in snow views at Solang Valley, or walking past colonial-era churches in Shimla, a week spent in Himachal Pradesh creates memories that stay with you long after the mountain trip ends. Pack your bags, keep your permits handy, and get ready for an unforgettable Himalayan road trip!

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. How many days are ideal for a Shimla and Manali trip?

A minimum of 7 days and 6 nights is ideal to cover both Shimla and Manali comfortably. This allows 2 nights in Shimla, 4 nights in Manali, and covers major day trips like Kufri, Solang Valley, Atal Tunnel, Sissu, and Naggar Castle without excessive rushing.

2. What is the best month to visit Shimla and Manali?

  • March to June: Best for pleasant spring/summer weather, sightseeing, and escaping city heat (temperatures range between 15°C and 28°C).
  • December to February: Best for catching live snowfall, snow sports in Solang Valley, and winter landscapes in Kufri and Manali.

3. Do I need a permit to visit Atal Tunnel and Sissu?

No, Indian citizens do not require a special permit to drive through the Atal Tunnel to Sissu or Lahaul Valley. However, if you plan to visit Rohtang Pass, a valid daily online permit from rohtangpermits.hp.gov.in is mandatory.

4. How much does a 7-day Shimla-Manali trip cost per person?

  • Budget Travelers: ₹12,000 – ₹18,000 per person (using public Volvo buses, budget guesthouses, and local dhabas).
  • Mid-Range Travelers: ₹20,000 – ₹35,000 per person (using private cabs, 3-star hotels, and casual cafe dining).
  • Luxury Travelers: ₹45,000+ per person (luxury heritage stays, private SUV rentals, and fine dining).

5. Is it safe to drive self-driven cars from Delhi to Shimla and Manali?

Yes, the NH5 and NH21 highways connecting Delhi to Shimla and Manali are well-maintained. However, driving on winding mountain roads requires confidence. Avoid night driving, take caution during monsoon heavy rainfall (July–August), and carry snow chains during peak winter months (Jan–Feb).

6. Is snowfall guaranteed in Manali and Shimla during winter?

Snowfall is never guaranteed on exact dates, but the highest chance to experience live snowfall in Shimla (Kufri) and Manali (Solang Valley / Atal Tunnel) is between late December and mid-February.

7. Which is better for shopping: Shimla or Manali?

Both offer unique regional items:

  • Shimla (Mall Road & Lakkar Bazaar): Best for wooden handicrafts, Himachali caps, jams, and colonial antiques.
  • Manali (Old Manali & Mall Road): Best for Kullu shawls, woolen socks, bohemian clothing, silver jewelry, and mountain trout.

8. What clothing should I pack for a 7-day trip?

  • Summer (Apr–Jun): Light cottons for daytime, with a light sweater or fleece jacket for cool evenings.
  • Winter (Nov–Feb): Heavy thermal innerwear, down jackets, woolen caps, waterproof gloves, and sturdy boots.

9. Can we cover Shimla and Manali by Volvo bus?

Yes, overnight Volvo buses run daily from Delhi (ISBT Kashmiri Gate) and Chandigarh to both Shimla and Manali. You can travel from Delhi to Shimla by bus, take a local inter-city bus from Shimla to Manali, and return to Delhi via an overnight Volvo from Manali.

10. How far is Atal Tunnel from Manali, and how long does it take?

The South Portal of the Atal Tunnel is approximately 25 km from Manali town and takes around 45 minutes to 1 hour to reach via Solang Valley, depending on road traffic.
